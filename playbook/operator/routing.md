# Routing doctrine

> DRAFT — pending field trial; see README.md in this directory.

How the operator navigates the corpus so the owner never has to. Three
facts make this cheap: every chapter shares one 13-section skeleton; the
gates and trees are already written (each chapter's §7, and all of
ch. 14); and the ladder (ch. 07) plus the taxonomy (ch. 03) key
diagnosis to intervention. Routing is lookup, not invention.

## Position notation

`NN§S` — chapter and section, plus the finer handle when one exists:
`07§5.1 rung 2 (entry evidence pending)`, `03§7 gate 2 FAILED`,
`04§5 contract frozen`. Every ledger row that moves the engagement
carries the position it moved.

## The chapter skeleton, operationally

| § | What it is | Operator use |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Purpose, inputs, decisions supported | Confirm the chapter applies before entering it |
| 4 | Normative principles | Constraints to honor; quote the one that binds, never the list |
| 5 | Default procedure | **The thing you actually run** — walk it stage by stage |
| 6 | Project adaptation parameters | Where the profile's answers change defaults |
| 7 | Decision gates and stopping conditions | **The thing you actually check** — every gate outcome is a ledger row |
| 8 | Metrics and formulas | Compute, show the number and the formula name |
| 9 | Failure modes and anti-patterns | Check before declaring a stage done |
| 10 | Vendor recipes | Packaged tools (handoffs live here — e.g. wikiskills-lab in ch. 07) |
| 11 | Worked examples | Surface one when the owner wants to see the destination |
| 12 | Outputs and artifacts | What must exist in `artifacts/` before the position advances |
| 13 | Sources | Cite when the owner asks "says who?" |

## Entry defaults

1. **Intake first** (`interview.md` → ch. 01). No engagement without a
   driving decision.
2. **The eval-foundation gate is always next** (ch. 03) unless the
   ledger already records passing evidence for the current suite version
   and execution-system identity. No optimization, training, or
   procurement question is routable past a failing ch. 03 gate — that is
   the corpus's own first principle, enforced, not re-argued.
3. **Then route by the presenting problem** through ch. 14's decision
   trees, and inside optimization work, by the ch. 07 ladder with its
   entry/exit evidence bars and the RC taxonomy (ch. 03). The ladder's
   discipline transfers whole: a rung whose entry evidence is absent is
   inapplicable, not exhausted; inapplicability is recorded, never
   assumed.

## How a recommendation is presented

Always this shape, and only this shape:

> Position: `03§5 stage 2`. Recommendation: draw a 15-task suite from
> the last month's real tickets with deterministic scorers, because the
> driving decision needs a baseline nothing else can give. Cost shape:
> about two sessions, no model spend beyond a dry run. Approve, or
> steer?

One recommendation, one sentence of reasoning, the cost shape, an
approve-or-steer. Alternatives exist only if the owner asks. The choice
— including "you pick" — becomes a `decision` ledger row.

## Rules

- **Never assign a chapter.** Surface the applicable slice (a §5 stage,
  a §7 gate, a ch. 14 checklist item) in one paragraph, with the citation
  so the owner can go deeper by choice.
- **Follow cross-references yourself.** When a procedure invokes another
  chapter (ch. 07 leaning on ch. 04's contract machinery, ch. 02's
  identity checks), fetch the requirement and bring it back; do not
  bounce the owner between chapters.
- **Gates produce rows, always** — pass, fail, or inapplicable-with-
  evidence. A gate cleared by silence is the anti-pattern the ladder
  names.
- **Templates are filled by conversation** (the interview pattern), then
  frozen per their own rules. The template file is canonical; the
  conversation is just how it gets filled.
- **Verdict vocabulary is exact** (ch. 04): CONFIRMED / REFUTED /
  INCONCLUSIVE, with INCONCLUSIVE re-scoping rather than exhausting.

**Date:** 2026-08-19 (all sources fetched live on this date unless noted)
**Method:** 13 parallel deep-research agents (one per research question; RQ2 split), ~370 sources opened across docs.nvidia.com, developer.nvidia.com, build.nvidia.com, github.com/NVIDIA\*, and partner sites; followed by an adversarial verification pass (5 verifiers re-fetching primary sources for every load-bearing single-source claim) and a gap-fill pass for three uncovered matrix rows. ## 1. Executive answer

**Is NVIDIA already publishing the playbook we need? No — but the answer changed shape in mid-2026, and the corrected claim matters.**

NVIDIA does **not** publish one coherent end-to-end methodology for the loop *requirement → trustworthy eval → model comparison → inference characterization → optimization → prompt/RAG-vs-finetune decision → data curation → training → post-tune eval → serving → local-vs-cloud-vs-API economics → deploy → observe → feed failures back*. No document connects those stages with decision gates. The "AI factory" lifecycle is datacenter infrastructure positioning that explicitly delegates AgentOps/observability/MLOps to partners (Enterprise AI Factory Design Guide, updated 2026-05-27), and the DGX Spark corpus is 46 excellent but independent install-and-verify tutorials with **no evaluation playbook and no sequencing** ([build.nvidia.com/spark](https://build.nvidia.com/spark), designated "the single source of truth for your Spark device").

What NVIDIA does supply falls into three strata:

1. **Per-stage execution recipes — STRONG and current.** Serving (vLLM/SGLang/TRT-LLM/llama.cpp/NIM playbooks, updated through Aug 2026), quantization (Model Optimizer, NVFP4 on-device in ~2h), fine-tuning mechanics (Unsloth/LLaMA Factory/NeMo AutoModel/PyTorch playbooks), data tooling (Curator, Data Designer), guardrails, deployment. Tutorials end at "loss decreases" or "server responds"; none evaluates what it built.
2. **One genuinely mature measurement methodology — inference performance.** The NIM LLM Benchmarking Guide (updated 2026-07-20, now AIPerf-based) plus the four-part 2025 blog series is a real, formula-level method: metric definitions (TTFT, ITL=(e2e−TTFT)/(tokens−1), system-vs-per-user TPS), use-case ISL/OSL pairs, concurrency-over-request-rate sweeps, operating-point selection on the latency-throughput curve, sizing, and $/M-token TCO. This is the strongest documented methodology anywhere in the corpus — but it measures **performance, not quality**.
3. **A brand-new (June–Aug 2026) decision layer — real, but weeks old and partly pre-alpha.** Three artifacts reverse what would have been "ABSENT" verdicts a few months ago:
   - **NeMo Evaluator SDK 0.3.0** (2026-06-03): `nel compare` (paired per-item McNemar test with power analysis — it explicitly warns a 164-item HumanEval can only detect ~22% regressions) and `nel gate` (YAML policy tiers → GO/NO-GO with 95% CIs, INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE under 10 paired items). NVIDIA's first statistical evaluation methodology — productized from an Amazon Science ICLR 2026 paper, and **not** propagated to the enterprise NeMo Microservices Evaluator, agent evals, or NVIDIA's own blogs (SkillEvaluator, published 2026-08-19, declines to report confidence intervals).
   - **NeMo Switchyard** (2026-08-11, pre-alpha, "not for production use"): official weak/strong model routing across local endpoints (vLLM/NIM/Ollama) and frontier APIs (OpenAI + Anthropic protocol translation; a launcher runs Claude Code through the router). Four documented decision mechanisms with parameters and fail-open semantics. This is NVIDIA validating the FIS cascade thesis — 8 days old at research time.
   - **NeMo Platform** (repo created 2026-05-14, active): the first integrated lifecycle artifact sized for a small team — evaluate/secure/tune (Switchyard routing under "Tune"), Harbor-backed regression suites, runs locally via Docker against **local Ollama or Anthropic/OpenAI/Gemini providers**, and is explicitly "driven from coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor)". Agent-centric, Studio UI alpha, no mandated sequence or decision rules.

**What must still come from outside NVIDIA** (the durable gaps, §7): experimental design (futility/sequential stopping — nothing anywhere; NVIDIA gives you the dashboard, not the tripwire), token-budget calibration as a procedure, building trustworthy eval sets from a requirement, acceptance thresholds (every NVIDIA eval artifact deliberately "doesn't decide pass/fail"), design-time model selection, local-vs-cloud-vs-API economics (pure positioning; every substantive TCO analysis found was third-party), judge calibration, prompt-change evaluation, three-tier routing as a worked reference, and a stated observability minimum for experimentation.

**Bottom line for the main deliverable lens:** FIS is **not** reinventing a workflow NVIDIA has codified. NVIDIA ships increasingly accessible building blocks and, since mid-2026, has begun codifying fragments of the decision layer (evaluate-first ordering, statistical regression gates, escalation routing). The experimental discipline — stopping rules, measurement validity, eval-set trust, promotion criteria — still has to be assembled from ML/eval research and the team's own practice. On several axes (futility stopping, token-budget calibration, cap-hit monitoring, cross-platform measurement validity) FIS's existing practice is **ahead of anything NVIDIA publishes**. The highest-value move is not adoption of an NVIDIA process (there isn't one) but selective adoption of NVIDIA's newest decision-layer artifacts into the FIS playbook — see §9.

## 2. The special DGX Spark question: what is NVIDIA teaching a Spark owner to DO?

**A. Product positioning.** Launch (2025-03-18 announcement, 2025-10-13 ship): "world's smallest AI supercomputer" — prototype/fine-tune/infer locally (1 PFLOP FP4, 128 GB unified, inference to ~200B params, fine-tune to 70B), then "seamlessly move models … to DGX Cloud or any accelerated cloud or data center infrastructure — with virtually no code changes." By mid-2026 the product page repositioned to **"a complete platform for local autonomous agents"** and softened migration to "conveniently evaluate the work for eventual migration." The develop-local/deploy-cloud story and the never-leave-the-device story now coexist unreconciled ([product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/dgx-spark/), fetched 2026-08-19).

**B. Concrete documented workflows.** 46 Spark playbooks (66 repo directories including 19 `station-*` DGX Station GB300 playbooks — a counting trap) at [github.com/NVIDIA/dgx-spark-playbooks](https://github.com/NVIDIA/dgx-spark-playbooks), mirrored at build.nvidia.com/spark, regenerated near-daily, dates Oct 2025–Aug 2026. Analyst-imposed taxonomy (NVIDIA exposes none): inference & serving (~11), optimization (2: NVFP4 quantization, speculative decoding), fine-tuning (5), multi-node networking (4), access/management (7), agents & applications (~12), domain workloads (5). All are install-verify-cleanup tutorials with troubleshooting tables. **No evaluation playbook exists**; no playbook sequences into another.

**C. Performance engineering guidance.** One real measurement document exists and it is buried: the **DGX Spark User Performance Guide** ([performance_benchmarking_guide.md](https://github.com/NVIDIA/dgx-spark-playbooks/blob/main/nvidia/connect-two-sparks/assets/performance_benchmarking_guide.md), an *asset inside the connect-two-sparks playbook*, not a catalog entry) — offline vs online benchmark procedure for all four engines (trtllm-bench, `vllm bench`, SGLang bench, llama.cpp) with ttft/tpot/itl/e2el percentile metrics, single-user decode at concurrency=1, fine-tuning and image-gen benchmarks, and dual-Spark NCCL/RoCE measurement. It has **no acceptance criteria, no expected numbers, and no accuracy component**, and pins containers two versions behind the current playbooks. The DGX Spark Porting Guide (PDF, 2026-05-24) adds genuine profiling discipline (Nsight over SSH, microbenchmark hygiene, the UMA `cudaMemGetInfo` under-reporting trap). The only explicit acceptance threshold in the entire Spark corpus is network-layer: NVIDIA Sync Cluster Assistant's 184+ Gbit/s link-speed check.

**D. Fine-tuning / customization guidance.** Five playbooks (Unsloth, PyTorch full/LoRA/QLoRA incl. two-Spark FSDP, NeMo AutoModel, LLaMA Factory, FLUX.1 Dreambooth). Success criterion is literally "loss decreasing over 60 steps" (Unsloth playbook); no playbook evaluates the resulting model; no hyperparameter methodology; no framework-selection criteria (the only "choosing a fine-tuning framework" page a searcher finds is an AI-generated third-party DeepWiki page). Fine-tunable-size claims are mutually inconsistent across NVIDIA-endorsed materials (70B launch / 120B Mar-2026 blog / 200B Unsloth partner guide). The only end-to-end Spark experiment with real before/after evaluation (synthetic CoT → TorchTune full FT → BFCLv3/v4) is on **pytorch.org** (2026-02-02), not an NVIDIA property.

**E. Production transition guidance.** Thin to absent. Substance behind "prototype → production": multi-arch NGC containers (same tags on ARM SBSA and x86 — a real, documented guarantee), HF/LoRA/GGUF artifact portability, cuTile/Tile IR kernel portability (with per-platform tile retuning), and `register-to-brev` (which brings the Spark *into* Brev's console for sharing — it does not migrate workloads *out*). **No playbook walks a fine-tuned model or serving stack from Spark to datacenter production.** NIM on Spark uses Spark-specific image variants ("Not every NIM has a DGX Spark variant"); NVFP4 checkpoints produced on Spark run only on Blackwell — not the H100s that dominate partner clouds. "DGX Cloud" itself has been repositioned as NVIDIA's internal environment; the rentable destination is now DGX Cloud Lepton / Brev, neither of which documents a local on-ramp.

**F. Experimental-science guidance. ABSENT.** Nothing in the Spark corpus teaches experiment design, stopping criteria, ablation practice, comparison methodology, or whether to fine-tune vs RAG vs prompt. The NVFP4 playbook says "we recommend running evaluations to verify … acceptable performance" and provides no tool, procedure, or threshold.

**Verdict: NVIDIA gives the prosumer AI engineer powerful tools plus performance-measurement mechanics, and no experimental discipline.** The discipline layer — exactly what FIS practices — is the part NVIDIA leaves to the owner.

## 7. Methodology gap analysis — what NVIDIA does NOT tell you how to do

Ordered by how much each gap costs a small evaluation-driven lab:

1. **Sequential/futility stopping of evaluations.** Nothing, anywhere. Quality Gates and `nel compare` are post-run. NVIDIA's tooling would have *prevented most of* the R6-style burn through defaults (10-sample pilots, ≥32K reasoning caps, 128-way parallelism, live running-accuracy, finish_reason stats, cheap resume) — but no NVIDIA source supplies the mid-run abort rule. **External fill (named):** Wald SPRT or group-sequential futility bounds (conditional power; O'Brien-Fleming/Pocock) on per-case pass/fail streams; ASHA/Hyperband (via Optuna pruners or Ray Tune) for sweep arms. NVIDIA ships adjacent Optuna integrations (Model Analyzer alpha, NAT Optimizer), so this extends NVIDIA-sanctioned tooling rather than departing from it. (The only full HPO methodology NVIDIA publishes — Hyperband/ASHA/BOHB/PBT with selection guidance — is in TAO Toolkit, which is computer-vision-only.)
2. **Token-budget calibration.** NVIDIA publishes per-benchmark exemplar values (1,024 code; 32,768–131,072 reasoning) and ships finish-reason statistics collection, but no "pilot the generation-length distribution, set cap above P99, alarm on truncation rate" procedure. FIS's R7 plan is ahead of NVIDIA here; keep it.
3. **Building a trustworthy evaluation from a requirement.** Eval-set construction, ground-truth curation, stratification, leakage protection for *your own* eval sets (NVIDIA covers only public-benchmark n-gram decontamination, for pretraining corpora), acceptance thresholds. Contamination is "named as a challenge" (Jan 2025 blog) with no procedure.
4. **Acceptance/promotion criteria.** Systematic pattern: every NVIDIA evaluation artifact refuses the decision. The Platform agent-eval doc says it verbatim: the evaluator "produces scores, aggregates, and provenance — it doesn't decide pass/fail, gate a release, or compare runs." The flywheel's rule was "flashlight, not autopilot." Only `nel gate` crosses into decision territory — and its thresholds are user-supplied. FIS must own its thresholds; NVIDIA now at least provides the statistical machinery to enforce them.
5. **Local-vs-cloud-vs-API economics.** Zero methodology; datacenter TCO formulas assume $320K 8-GPU servers. The LangChain break-even formula (min offload = judge_cost/(strong−weak)) is the single quantitative routing-economics rule in the corpus, and it's a partner post.
6. **Design-time model selection.** Catalogs + eval tooling + runtime routing; no shortlisting procedure against a requirement.
7. **Judge calibration.** Operational judge mechanics documented; no human-agreement statistics, kappa, sample sizes, or bias audits anywhere.
8. **Prompt-change evaluation.** Structurally excluded: `nel compare` *requires identical prompt templates* (prompt changes are confounds). No A/B methodology for prompts exists.
9. **Three-tier routing.** All shipped Switchyard algorithms are two-tier; the N-target "custom" mode requires a user-written policy selector; no worked three-tier config exists. Also: no routing-quality monitoring/regression methodology post-deployment.
10. **Cross-platform measurement validity.** Nothing says whether evals measured on Spark (NVFP4, unified memory) remain valid on H100/FP8 cloud targets. For an evaluation-driven team, migration silently invalidates prior results until re-run — NVIDIA never mentions it. (Second-order corollary NVIDIA also never states: **choose quantization format for the deployment target, not the dev box** — NVFP4 artifacts don't execute on Hopper.)
11. **Experimentation observability minimum.** Production serving has a de facto documented minimum (Prometheus + OTel + JSONL + DCGM + Grafana); single-box experimentation has tool availability and no stated practice. On GB10 specifically, memory observability is worse than on an RTX 5080 (NVML by-design broken, dcgm-exporter misreports, Nsight UVM unsupported).
12. **Statistical treatment of stochastic agents.** Trajectory tooling is rich; repeats/variance/CI guidance absent; NVIDIA's own SkillEvaluator (2026-08-19) ran 85% of skills at one attempt and declines CIs — the statistical layer hasn't propagated even internally.
13. **Progressive delivery and lineage.** Live shadow/A-B testing: nothing (no Triton request mirroring; "A/B testing" appears once in the corpus, in a 2024 partner guest blog). Canary: only via the NIM Operator's KServe/Knative mode, mechanics delegated to Knative; native paths are plain rolling updates with rollback "manual if something goes wrong"; Dynamo lists percentage rollouts as Future Work. Lineage: two real registries (NGC Private Registry; NeMo Entity Store/Data Store with one-hop `base_model`/`peft` fields) but no prescribed dataset→run→checkpoint→eval→deployment chain; LoRA adapters are versionless directory names. The recurring pattern: mechanism documented, methodology delegated — to the Kubernetes ecosystem or to humans.

**External methodologies to pair with NVIDIA's stack** (minimal set): Anthropic-style "error bars on evals" practice (paired analysis, CIs, power); SPRT/group-sequential analysis; ASHA/Hyperband literature (Li et al.); lm-eval-harness stderr conventions; Amazon's LLM-Accuracy-Stats (the exact method `nel compare` productized — usable standalone on lm-eval output); tinyBenchmarks/IRT-style adaptive subsetting for progressive eval.

## 8. "If I own a DGX Spark, what process should I follow?"

The strongest process NVIDIA's **current material actually supports**, in order, with everything NVIDIA does not supply marked **[EXTERNAL]**. NVIDIA never writes this sequence down; every numbered step cites where its pieces live.

1. **Stand up the box and serving.** DGX OS + DGX Dashboard (localhost:11000); NVIDIA Sync or Tailscale for laptop access. Serve with the **vLLM playbook** (NGC container, ~30-model NVFP4/FP8 matrix) for the general case; llama.cpp playbook for GGUF/interactive; NIM playbook only if the enterprise contract matters (~5 GB10 models). No engine-selection rule exists — **[EXTERNAL: pick per measured ttft/tpot on your workload; FIS already does this]**.
2. **Baseline performance measurement.** Use the buried **DGX Spark User Performance Guide** verbatim: offline + online modes per engine, ttft/tpot/itl/e2el percentiles, concurrency=1 for the single-user regime; AIPerf against the running endpoint for sweeps (NIM Benchmarking Guide method: warm-up, request-count=3×concurrency, curve, operating point). Pin container versions per run — the guide itself pins stale ones. **[EXTERNAL: acceptance thresholds and run-to-run variance policy; NVIDIA gives GPU-hygiene hints (clocks/persistence) and AIPerf CIs only.]**
3. **Quantize deliberately.** NVFP4 playbook (W4A16 recipe for interactive/single-user; W4A4 for concurrency) or skip via NVIDIA's pre-quantized HF catalog (~80+ checkpoints with per-benchmark delta tables). Gate the result the way NVIDIA itself does but doesn't document: fix a benchmark suite, require **>99% median accuracy recovery vs the higher-precision baseline** to ship PTQ-only; escalate down the documented ladder (disable KV-quant → partial-NVFP4 variants → AWQ/AutoQuantize → QAD) below that. **[Boundary: the >99% rule is NVIDIA's demonstrated internal practice (Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, 2026-08-17), adopted here as policy — NVIDIA doctrine says only "run evaluations".]** Caveat: if the artifact might ever run on non-Blackwell cloud GPUs, quantize FP8/W4A16 instead — NVFP4 is Blackwell-only.
4. **Evaluate quality with the Evaluator SDK against the local endpoint.** `nel eval` with `--dry-run` then `limit_samples=10` pilot, then parallel full run with caching/resume; `nel compare` (paired McNemar) for any model/quantization/runtime swap; `nel gate` with critical/supporting/advisory tiers as the promotion gate. NVIDIA never bridges Evaluator to Spark — the bridge is simply "any OpenAI-compatible endpoint" and community evidence says 4 of 5 harnesses run on ARM64. **[EXTERNAL: your thresholds; futility stopping mid-run (SPRT); token-budget calibration from pilot P99 + truncation alarms; note `nel compare` cannot evaluate prompt changes.]**
5. **Fine-tune only after the evaluate-first gate.** Follow the May 2026 customization framework: prompt engineering + eval infrastructure + tool definitions first; train only where data shows the gap. Then: Unsloth or LLaMA Factory playbook (LoRA/QLoRA; LLaMA Factory is the only one with an export step), NeMo AutoModel for the NVIDIA-native path; dataset sizing per the RTX AI Garage thresholds (100–1,000 pairs PEFT / 1,000+ full FT) and the tool-calling tutorial's pattern (85/15 split + independent golden set; 500+ synthetic samples → ~93% on that task). Early stopping: Customizer defaults (val_loss, patience 10, min-delta 0.001) as the reference. Re-run step 4 before/after. **[EXTERNAL: leakage protection for your own eval sets; train/val/test ratios; curve interpretation — NVIDIA delegates that to Unsloth's docs.]**
6. **Build the failure→data loop from the deprecated blueprint's design.** Log production prompt/response with workload tags → stratified splits → base/ICL/LoRA experiment triple → judge-scored comparison → human-gated promotion ("flashlight, not autopilot"). The artifact is deprecated (Apr 2026); the loop design remains NVIDIA's best template and fits FIS's cascade-escalation harvesting. **[EXTERNAL: everything statistical about the promotion decision — use nel gate.]**
7. **Run agents sandboxed.** NemoClaw/OpenShell for credential custody + egress policy (demo-grade, add your own controls), or AI Workbench project containers for Claude Code/Cursor sandboxing (the freshest Workbench docs, Aug 2026). Store trajectories in ATIF via NeMo Agent Toolkit / Relay; profile with `nat` profiler.
8. **Route across tiers with Switchyard semantics.** Escalation router (weak-first, judge on actual output, confirmations=2, latch, fail-open) between the local specialist and the frontier API; validate economics with the break-even rule (route only if achievable offload > judge_cost/(strong−weak)). Pre-alpha — treat as reference semantics, not production dependency. Three-tier = chain two routes or write a custom policy selector — **[EXTERNAL: assembled, not published; plus all routing-quality monitoring]**.
9. **Observe.** vLLM Prometheus metrics (`vllm:*` — the vocabulary NIM and Dynamo pass through), W3C traceparent end-to-end, DGX Dashboard for eyeballs; budget for a custom /proc/meminfo collector because NVML/DCGM memory metrics are broken on GB10 **[community precedent, no official fix]**.
10. **Reproducibility.** Pin everything the Nemotron eval recipes pin (containers, sampling params, judges, harness versions); keep environments as Workbench projects or versioned Dockerfiles; publish eval recipes alongside results.

What NVIDIA-only process **cannot** give you at any step: the decision to stop, the decision to promote, the decision to buy (local vs API), and confidence that a number measured on one tier means anything on another. Those four remain FIS's own discipline.

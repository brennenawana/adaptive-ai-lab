## 0. Executive summary

**Q1 — The methodology exists, and it is not NVIDIA's.** The gaps the NVIDIA audit identified — futility stopping, token-budget calibration, eval-set trust, acceptance statistics, judge calibration — are covered by mature non-NVIDIA sources: sequential analysis and clinical-trial adaptive design (the direct answer to the frozen-contract-vs-early-stopping tension: **pre-register the rule, not the outcome**), Miller 2024 ("Adding Error Bars to Evals") for reporting statistics, racing/successive-halving for arm screening, and a 2024–26 LLM-eval literature (EvalGen, GSM1k, JudgeBench, τ-bench) that in several places *vindicates* FIS's existing practice as ahead of common guidance. The R6 diagnosis, after independent statistical re-derivation against FIS's own trajectory store (§2.2, Appendix C): **it was a decision-quality failure, not a stopping-rule failure.** The pilot detected the broken cap; the pre-registered tolerance carried no consequence. The corrected fixes: consequence-bearing tolerances with curtailed exact counting at TRAIN (halt at the 4th cap-hit — dominates the SPRT on FIS's real data), an assumption-free certainty-curtailment clause for hopeless arms (worth ~2–4% of wall as protection, **not** the ~10h a naive reading suggests — the 9.14h burn sat inside a *viable* arm and is a throughput/science problem for R7 and hardware), and cluster-robust statistics that reveal the sharpest new fact of this study: **at the measured class clustering, Suite v3's TEST has an effective N of ~22 — R6's own +13.5pp headline comparison is not statistically significant cluster-robustly.** Suite v3 supports gross gates and certain-bound exclusions, not fine model ranking; power grows by adding *classes*, not replications.

**Q2 — Hardware: buy nothing this week; the draft build was refuted by its own evidence.** The Aug-2026 market verification found prices *above* even the pessimistic first pass (RTX 5090 in-stock floor ≈ $4,900; used 4090 $3.6K+; DGX Spark now $4,999; DDR5 96GB ≈ $1,900 — a documented, multi-outlet AI memory crunch with three NVIDIA hikes in 2026 and press forecasting two more years of DRAM scarcity). The adversarial review then reconstructed R6's critical path from the autopsy's own timing CSV and showed the *second* purchased GPU saves 0.0 minutes (one added node reaches the 13.67h floor with 4.87h slack), the lab has ~38 lifetime GPU-hours (≈$19 rented), and the autopsy's #1 action — run R7 before any hardware decision — was being contradicted. The adopted recommendation (§3.5): a **staged, trigger-based plan** — a ~$4 rental benchmark this week to measure the one number the decision turns on (3090 and 5090 decode on the pinned FIS artifact), rented second-node arms at ~$5–12/milestone capturing the full measured 8.8h overlap saving, a pre-committed purchase trigger (3 consecutive months >$150/mo rented spend, or a committed always-on serving tier), and a designated buy when it fires (one GPU on a minimal host — not two, not a $2.6K DDR5 platform). DGX Spark's rejection got stronger: $4,999 verified, partner boxes $4,661+, and 273 GB/s remains a decode downgrade vs. hardware already owned. Conditions under which Spark becomes correct are stated (§3.5.4).

**Q3 — The two-level laboratory is right; its correct implementation is inside FIS.** Strategy C in structure, delivered as formalized decision rules rather than a named parallel tier (the adversarial review's sharpening, adopted): **consequence-bearing tolerances** (every pre-registered tolerance names ABORT / RECALIBRATE / PROCEED-WITH-DECLARED-CEILING, enforced fail-closed in the runner — the actual R6 fix), a **SMOKE state** in the existing R6 registry (36 stratified cases, single arm, results ledgered and cryptographically non-promotable — the cheap tier, without a hole in the fail-closed machinery), full-corpus static gates explicitly protected from subsetting, an **elimination rule** (no candidate withdrawn on a pilot margin below the pilot's own MDE — the UD-quant withdrawal at McNemar p≈0.22–0.48 is the precedent being corrected), a **TEST-look ledger** (Suite v3's TEST has been evaluated seven times; a pre-registered Suite-v4 refresh trigger is now due), and exactly one genuinely separate artifact: a **throwaway fine-tuning pipeline rig**, because FIS has no training code and its provenance schema cannot yet represent a self-produced model. A separate general-purpose minimal system stays rejected: FIS's most valuable discoveries required realism and class breadth; its iteration speed is not the binding constraint (E6 variants ~10 min; the 24h outlier decomposes into fixable causes).

**Q4 — Do next** (full model + ten prioritized actions, §5): M0 first — a ~half-day truncation diagnostic (persist `reasoning_content`, classify the known cap-hit cases as convergent-vs-degenerate, run the context fit-probe) plus the $4 GPU benchmark; then the contract-template upgrade (M-STAT); then R7 re-scoped by M0's answer; the FT pipeline rig before any R8; hardware purchase only on trigger.

## 1. What the FIS record establishes (evidence base for everything below)

Read in full for this study: `experiment-log.md`, `HANDOFF.md`, `R6_PERFORMANCE_AUTOPSY.md`, `R6_MODERN_LOCAL_SPECIALIST_REFRESH_REPORT.md`, plus the Suite-v3/R5 records and the milestone timing entries in `OVERNIGHT_STATUS.md`. The facts that drive the recommendations:

**Iteration-speed facts.** E6 prompt variants: ~10 min each, run serially because each variant's design depended on the last result. R3/R3b (model + budget factor, dev-only): hours each. Suite v3 full three-arm re-baseline: ~4.6 h of runs. R5 (learned routing, TRAIN acquisition + replays): ~4h15m. R6 (4 candidates, 516 cases): **24.1 h**, of which 93.4% was serial local decode, 65% one dense-27B family at ~28 tok/s, and **41% (9 h 55 m) cap-hit truncation that scored zero** (145 cases, 0 passes). The whole experimental program to date spans five days of wall clock.

**The R6 failure, precisely.** The TRAIN pilot (36 stratified cases) *measured* truncation of 11–42% per candidate at the 8,192 cap. The contract's calibration rule ("smallest cap meeting ≤3/36 truncations") had no admissible cap, so 8,192 was frozen "with truncation recorded." No abort mechanism existed downstream, by design — the freeze protects comparability. DEV and TEST then ran to completion. This is a *detection success and a decision failure*: the information existed at pilot time; the contract had no pre-registered rule that could act on it, and no pre-registered rule that could stop an arm mid-run once cap-hits were producing deterministic zeros.

**The hardware facts.** RTX 5080 Laptop 16 GB: measured effective decode bandwidth 353–496 GB/s (39–55% of ~896 GB/s spec); 27B dense Q3 = 12.6–15.2 cases/hour; 16 GB forced strict arm serialization (Q3_K_M used 96% of the card) and capped context headroom (a 12,288 cap at `-c 16384` "barely fits"); zero retries/errors across 516 cases; GPU 93%-busy across the 24 h. The autopsy's own counterfactuals: a second inference node saves 5–9 h on an R6-shaped milestone (SAFE NOW — per-candidate execution systems are already machine-specific); a DGX Spark as *replacement* would have roughly doubled R6 (273 GB/s < achieved effective bandwidth); only more bandwidth or fewer tokens compresses the dominant chain.

**The discipline facts.** Thirteen harness/scenario defects were initially indistinguishable from model weakness and were found by three detection mechanisms — measured evidence-reachability ceilings, weak-beats-strong inversions, cross-arm disagreement review — all of which are *statistical signals requiring breadth* (many cases, many classes, multiple arms). R5's learned-routing null was itself a detection win: leave-one-class-out analysis plus an invented class-identity ceiling showed the router was learning *which template it was*. Reproducibility is same-session-only (23/48 case outcomes moved across a mere server restart), measured and then enforced by rule. Pre-registered adoption rules prevented at least two cherry-picks (E6b's H variant; R3b's verdict).

## 2. Q1 — The best non-NVIDIA methodology

### 2.1 The assembled methodology, by lifecycle area

Each item names the source, what it actually establishes, its status (consensus / strong-evidence / useful-heuristic / contested / research-only), and what FIS should do. Statuses and labels are the research agents', spot-checked; Tier-1 items passed adversarial review (Appendix C).

**Requirement → evaluation claim.**
- Anthropic's eval-design docs (fetched 2026-08-20): multidimensional explicit criteria, grader type chosen by task shape, edge-case checklists — *consensus*. FIS's per-dimension conjunctive scoring already matches structurally.
- OpenAI eval guidance: define objective → dataset → metrics → run → continuous; **validate any model-grader against human labels before trusting it** — *consensus*.
- Hamel Husain's error-analysis-first process (read dozens of traces, open-ended failure notes, then synthesize a taxonomy; NurtureBoss case: 3 failure modes = 60% of problems) — *useful-heuristic*, and functionally what FIS's arm-disagreement reviews already do.
- **EvalGen / "Who Validates the Validators" (Shankar et al., arXiv:2404.12272)**: *criteria drift* — users cannot fully specify eval criteria before seeing outputs; criteria and ground truth co-evolve — *strong-evidence* (n=9 practitioner study). This is the single most load-bearing external validation of FIS's frozen-suite + versioned-release discipline: v1→v2→v3 with cross-suite refusal is precisely the guard that makes criteria drift explicit and auditable instead of silent. FIS's 13 harness defects *are* criteria drift caught by instrument rather than by anecdote.

**Eval-set construction and ground truth.**
- SWE-bench Verified (OpenAI 2024): 1,699 instances human-reviewed → 500 kept; GPT-4o solve rate 16%→33.2% purely from removing unsolvable/mislabeled tasks — *strong-evidence*, and the exact external analog of FIS's reachability-ceiling work at 30× the scale. FIS's mechanism (measured ceilings, inversions) is arguably more rigorous than SWE-bench's annotator judgment.
- Anthropic's own guidance prefers *volume over hand-curation* ("more questions with slightly lower-signal automated grading is better") — *useful-heuristic in tension with FIS's design*; FIS's choice is the SWE-bench-Verified-style choice and should be stated as deliberate.

**Leakage and contamination.**
- GSM1k (arXiv:2405.00332): up to 8 pp accuracy drop on a fresh parallel benchmark; contamination correlates (r²=0.36) but doesn't fully explain overfitting — *strong-evidence*. FIS's disjoint seed ranges match GSM1k's held-out philosophy and are sound *while the corpus stays private*.
- BIG-bench canary strings (arXiv:2206.04615 §2.4): a distinct, cooperative-scraper convention for *published* task text — *useful-heuristic*. **New action for FIS:** add a canary GUID to TEST scenario files and any published excerpts — cheap insurance if the repo or reports ever become public. Previously unaddressed.

**Deterministic graders vs LLM judges, and judge calibration.**
- Zheng et al. (MT-Bench): GPT-4-judge ~80% agreement with humans **on open-ended chat preference** — *strong-evidence, wrong domain for FIS*.
- **JudgeBench (arXiv:2410.12784, numbers verified from the paper's tables, not the summarizer):** on objectively verifiable tasks, GPT-4o-as-judge scores ~56.6% (near chance on a binary task); best tested model 80.9%; some fine-tuned judges below chance — *strong-evidence*. **FIS's all-deterministic grading is vindicated for exactly its domain class.** Not a gap; ahead of default practice.
- "Reliability without Validity" (arXiv:2606.19544, 21 judges, 541K judgments): chance-corrected κ deflates apparent judge quality 33–41 pp; judges can be stably biased (test-retest >0.95 with position bias >0.10) — *contested/recent*. If FIS ever adds a judge to close the verifier-blind-spot, the bar is a κ-corrected, bias-audited protocol against human labels — never a raw agreement rate.

**Stochastic-agent evaluation.**
- pass@k unbiased estimator (Chen et al. 2021) vs **τ-bench's pass^k** (arXiv:2406.12045): gpt-4o agent at pass^1 >60% collapses below 25% at pass^8 on identical tasks — *strong-evidence*. FIS reports pass^1 only. **New action:** measure pass^k (k=3–5) for the frontier arm on a TEST subset — cheap relative to local inference, and the cascade's reliability claims are otherwise single-look artifacts. (The frontier arm is already known non-reproducible; this quantifies it.)
- Nondeterminism at T=0: Thinking Machines' batch-invariance work (1,000 identical prompts → 80 distinct completions; fix costs 60–110% throughput in vLLM, ~34% in SGLang's independent implementation) plus arXiv:2506.09501 (reduction-order sensitivity varies with GPU/runtime state, no concurrency needed) — *strong-evidence*. The latter is the better-fitting mechanism for FIS's measured restart-to-restart flips; FIS's single-slot same-session rule targets the dominant variance source and is the standard-shaped solution (vLLM ships batch-invariance only as opt-in beta). llama.cpp's maintainer notes KV-cache placement as a second-order factor — the rule is necessary, not sufficient, for bit-identity; FIS's outcome-level same-session discipline already accounts for this.

**Statistical reporting (the largest pure gap in FIS practice).**
- **Miller 2024 (arXiv:2411.00640, read in full):** SE formulas (Bernoulli only for 0/1 scores), **clustered SEs** (up to 3.05× naive on real evals — FIS's 12 scenario classes are a textbook clustering unit), **paired-difference SEs** (strictly tighter whenever arms' per-question scores correlate — FIS's same-seed cross-arm design gets this free and doesn't claim it), and the power formula whose worked example concludes ~**1,000 questions for a 3-pp MDE** — *strong-evidence*. Applied to FIS (McNemar-based, cross-validated by the research agent, independently re-derived in review): **DEV(48) resolves only ~16–22 pp paired differences; TEST(96) ~11–16 pp** (at discordance 0.15–0.30, α=.05, power .80). Consequences: (i) the "+2-case fluke" adoption rule is quantitatively validated; (ii) suite sizes are fit for gross configuration gates, not fine model ranking; (iii) **every contract should carry an MDE statement so a null is never read as equivalence** — R6's "47 vs 48 = competitive" reading was correct and should become the norm.

**Fine-tuning decisions and data.**
- RAG/prompt-first for factual/citation tasks: Ovadia (EMNLP 2024), Soudani, Balaguer — three studies agreeing in direction (with real FT value in some conditions; not "RAG always wins") — *strong-evidence*. Validates FIS's ladder ordering as practiced.
- Sizing: LIMA's n=1,000 is for *broad style alignment on a 65B base* — **no primary source exists for minimum-n on a narrow behavioral fix** (citation discipline). Genuine field gap; treat minimum-n as an ablation (e.g., 50/150/450 rungs against DEV under one-look discipline).
- LoRA/QLoRA: verified VRAM floors (QLoRA min: 9B ≈ 6.5 GB, 14B ≈ 8.5 GB, **27B ≈ 22 GB, 70B ≈ 41 GB**; 16-bit LoRA 27B ≈ 64 GB — Unsloth docs cross-checked against the QLoRA paper). Quality: Biderman ("LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less", single 7B model — scope caveat) and "LoRA Without Regret" (Thinking Machines 2025: all-layer LoRA at post-training scale ≈ full FT at ~67% FLOPs; explicitly reconciles with Biderman by regime) — *strong-evidence with stated limits*. Start: all-linear-layer LoRA, rank 16–32, α=2r, LR ~10× full-FT.
- Forgetting is real, scale-trends non-obvious, and model merging does not reliably fix it — *strong-evidence*. **FT gate must be the full TEST suite, never just the target-behavior slice.**
- **ToS constraint (Tier-1, verified from Anthropic's live legal pages):** Commercial Terms §D.4 (eff. 2025-06-17) and AUP (eff. 2025-09-15) prohibit using Claude inputs/outputs to train other models without prior authorization. **FIS's planned failure-harvest SFT is compliant as designed** — R5/R6 TRAIN trajectories are local-model traces scored against FIS's own ground truth — but any "distill the frontier arm" variant is off the table without authorization. Using Claude to *evaluate* is unaffected.
- Error-driven data loops (LoopTool, CurateEvo — 2025/26 preprints): promising, unreviewed; pattern matches FIS's design; pilot-grade only.

**Serving, routing, deployment ops.**
- **Cascade literature (all four majors read):** FrugalGPT (trained regression gate; up to 98% cost cut on a *financial-news* task), RouteLLM (~55K preference labels; near-random OOD without task-specific augmentation — the key caution for any learned router), AutoMix (noisy self-verification + POMDP, ≥50 labels), Hybrid LLM (one-shot predictive router). **None uses a deterministic rule-based gate. FIS's R4 is outside the published design space** — unvalidated by precedent in both directions, justified entirely by FIS's own numbers (which are strong: rescue ~100%, unnecessary 0), and genuinely novel. Worth a short public writeup, together with the Switchyard JSON-key-order/grammar finding, which no source documents (the general class is ML Test Score's "training/serving skew"; the transport-serialization instance appears unpublished).
- Shadow/canary: SageMaker shadow testing (mirroring, comparison left to user), Azure ML safe rollout (manual staged), Kayenta (automated statistical judge) — and **Google's SRE Workbook explicitly warns against over-investing in canary statistics** ("simplest model that meets your objectives"; one canary at a time; causally attributable metrics; aggregation window ≪ canary duration) — *consensus*. For a lab shipping to small clients: manual mirroring + a small causal metric set, not a Kayenta clone.
- ML Test Score (Breck et al., read in full): 28-test rubric, final score = *minimum* across categories; Monitor-3 ("training and serving compute the same values") is the named class of FIS's proxy-serialization bug; Monitor-7 (hold out annotated production examples for regression) is the failure-harvesting pattern — *consensus*. Adopt as an annual self-audit.
- Lineage tooling: MLflow/W&B/DVC provide versioning and lineage-by-reference; **none documents tamper-evidence of the historical record** — FIS's hash-chained registry is justified for exactly and only that property. Adopt MLflow/W&B for *metrics dashboards* if wanted; keep the registry for the record of record.

### 2.2 The R6 failure against established methodology — corrected by independent re-derivation

The first-pass analysis (research agent) proposed SPRT + curtailment + spending functions and claimed ~10h recoverable. The adversarial statistical review **re-derived everything against FIS's own trajectory store** (exact per-case cap-hit sequences from `learning.case_scores ⋈ learning.trajectories`, per-case wall times from the R6 report JSONs, ICC measured by ANOVA on real per-class indicators, 20–100k-replicate simulations) and materially corrected the picture. The corrected findings, each verified against real R6 data:

**Finding 1 — R6 was a decision-quality failure, not a stopping-rule failure.** The 36-case pilot measured a 5× tolerance breach (15/36 cap-hits vs ≤3/36 pre-registered) and the contract's own escape hatch said *proceed*. Detection was never the gap; the tolerance had no consequence attached. The fix is **consequence-bearing tolerances**: every pre-registered tolerance names its breach consequence — ABORT / RECALIBRATE / PROCEED-WITH-DECLARED-CEILING (with the projected cost stated) — enforced in-run by the runner, fail-closed, in the same style as the existing `--candidate` refusal. Under that rule the 9h55m would have been a *priced, chosen* cost or avoided — either way a decision, not a drift.

**Finding 2 — For threshold compliance, exact counting dominates the SPRT.** Replaying both rules on the four real pilot sequences: count-to-4 ("halt and escalate at the 4th cap-hit") fires at cases 17/16/21/35; the drafted SPRT fires later where it works (20/17), **accepts H0 wrongly at case 9** on a real 10/36 violator (qwen3.5-9B), and never fires on a real 4/36 violator (Bonsai). Worse, cap-hits are class-clustered (measured ICC 0.398) and the case order is class-blocked, so the SPRT's nominal α=.05 inflates to ~11% at the measured clustering. The i.i.d. "~7–15 cases" expectation is wrong by 2–2.5× on the real ordering. **Contract rule: curtailed exact counting, no hypothesis test, no error-rate claim.**

**Finding 3 — Certainty curtailment is correct, and worth ~2–4%, not ~10h.** The rule — abort an arm when `k_passes + cases_remaining < ⌈bar·N⌉` — is exact arithmetic, assumption-free under any ordering, and cannot inflate the arm's own type-I error (verified; with a 50% bar and zero passes it fires by case 49 of 96, as drafted). But replayed on the real R6 TEST arms it saves **0.51h of 24.05h**: Qwen3.8 (47/96, the arm that owned 13.7h of wall) sat *at* the bar and never curtails; only Bonsai fires (case 80, 16 cases saved). Structural reason: certainty-curtailment only fires on hopeless arms, and the arms that burn wall clock are the near-bar ones. **Adopt it as tail-risk protection and rigour, never as the speed story.** Three mandatory guards (all demonstrated on real data): (a) *spend semantics* — TEST is spent at the first executed case; curtailment is admissible only because its sole consequence is irreversible rejection; (b) *no partial point estimates* — report the certain interval `[k, k+remaining]/N` plus the unrun classes (Bonsai's partial 31/80=38.8% overstates its true 34/96=35.4% purely compositionally); (c) *paired-comparison firewall* — curtailed arms never enter paired comparisons: on real data, curtailing Bonsai at case 80 flips Qwen3.8-vs-Bonsai from McNemar p=0.0596 to p=0.0139 by deleting concordant-fail classes.

**Finding 4 — The 9.14h cap-hit burn is a throughput/science problem, and no stopping rule touches it.** 145 cap-hit cases (0 passes) sat *inside retained cases of viable arms*. A causally implementable per-case abort (using only reasoning-so-far; content follows reasoning, so mid-generation `content_chars=0` cannot distinguish a doomed case from a passing one) saves ~0.09h at zero pass loss. The burn routes to: R7 (convert truncations at a larger cap — costs *more* wall per case but buys signal — or adopt the 39%-faster UD quant once budget isn't binding) and the bandwidth lever (§3). **Contracts should state stopping rules are adopted for decision quality, explicitly not as a speed remedy.**

**Finding 5 — Cluster-robust statistics change what Suite v3 can claim.** Measured on the real 96 shared TEST cases: paired-difference ICC across the 12 classes = 0.475 → design effect 4.33 → SE inflation 2.08× → **effective N ≈ 22 pairs**. Corrected MDEs: unclustered TEST(96) 10.9–15.5pp (pd .15–.30) and DEV(48) 21.6pp at pd=.30 (*not detectable* at pd=.15 — the first-pass "16pp" was arithmetically impossible since MDE ≤ pd always); cluster-corrected, **nothing is detectable at pd ≤ .30, and the realized pd=0.427 gives ~37pp MDE**. Consequence, verified: R6's headline Qwen3.8-vs-Bonsai +13.5pp is *not significant* cluster-robustly (t=0.98, df=11), and the DEV gates qualified candidates at 1–2-case margins an order of magnitude inside their own MDE. **Mandatory disclosure going forward: Suite v3 at N=48/96 with 12 classes supports gross configuration gates, descriptive comparison, and certain-bound exclusion — not significance claims between local arms.** Deep design consequence: with high ICC, power is governed by the *number of classes*, not cases per class — Suite v4, when its trigger fires (§4), should add classes before adding replications.

**Finding 6 — One measurement-neutral cross-cutting fix: round-robin case ordering.** The class-*blocked* order (S01×8, S02×8, …) makes every run prefix unrepresentative (Qwen3.8's 24 consecutive failures arrive last) — the worst case for any interim rule. Deterministic round-robin interleaving makes prefix estimates 5.4× more accurate on the real data, changes no case/gold/scorer, and is a precondition for screening rungs meaning anything. (One caveat from R1: the first case in run order is sensitive to its prompt-cache predecessor; order changes only affect forward comparability, which is already session-scoped.)

**Finding 7 — What clinical trials actually contribute survives intact:** the FDA-definition resolution of the frozen-contract tension — *adaptation rules pre-specified before the trial; a DSMB executes the rule rather than the investigator's judgment*. That is exactly FIS's contract philosophy extended in-run. The specific conditional-power ~0.2 convention and Lan–DeMets spending options were **dropped**: the threshold could not be verified against a primary source this session, and any CP>0 rule reintroduces the i.i.d. model the measured clustering invalidates. The deterministic clause *is* the CP=0 case and needs no convention.

**Screening (pre-freeze only):** ASHA-style rungs (12→24→48, η=2, ~3× fewer case-executions across 4 candidates) are adopted for TRAIN screening with two corrections: rungs must be **class-balanced** (a 12-case rung in blocked order is 12 cases of one class, DEFF ~5–8, and inverts real candidate rankings on the R6 pilots), and Bernstein racing applies at class level (n=12) or not at all. Screening is ranking, never inference; it feeds the frozen comparison.

**What would NOT have prevented R6:** more pilot data (the pilot already measured it), a smaller suite (same absent rule, smaller burn — a cost reducer, not a fix), any post-run statistic, or — the correction this study itself needed — arm-level curtailment (the burn was inside a viable arm). The fixes are: one consequence clause at TRAIN, one curtailment clause with guards for tails, cluster-honest reporting, and R7 for the budget question itself.

## 4. Q3 — FIS vs a smaller testbed

### 4.1 The three strategies against the evidence

The evidence base is Appendix A (FIS history classified), the external testbed literature (W7), and an adversarial review that verified its claims against the repository itself (628 collected tests; contract §6/§7; `run_eval.py:381`; `provenance.py`; the Postgres trajectory store).

**Strategy B (temporary pivot to a minimal system) — rejected, strongest case first.** Steelman: a tiny lifecycle-complete system would exercise fine-tune→serve→observe loops FIS has never run, at seconds-per-experiment cost. Rebuttal: (i) the transfer-back assumption is precisely what R5's class-identity leakage demonstrated fails — method conclusions entangle with the world they were developed in; (ii) external transfer evidence (muP, scaling laws) validates small→large transfer only for smooth continuous metrics under matched parametrization, not discrete conjunctive pass/fail decisions; (iii) B abandons the standing regression value of the realistic suite and the fail-closed registry — the project's two hardest-to-rebuild assets. B is the weakest-supported option in both the external literature and the internal record.

**Strategy A (pure FIS) — nearly right, and its pure form hides the actual gaps.** FIS's iteration speed is not the binding constraint (E6 ~10 min/variant; Suite v3 re-baseline 4.6h; the 24h outlier decomposes into a bandwidth-bound chain + an unpriced tolerance breach). But pure A leaves four measured process gaps unfixed: consequence-free tolerances (R6), elimination decisions below pilot MDE (the UD-quant withdrawal at McNemar p≈0.22–0.48, on a metric confounded by the very cap R7 exists to fix — and made *permanent* by contract), TEST-split depletion with no family-wise accounting (seven evaluations of the same 96 cases to date), and no training pipeline at all (`ModelArtifact` cannot even represent a self-produced weight file — every field assumes a third-party download).

**Strategy C (two-level lab) — right structure, wrong default implementation.** The draft proposal ("FIS-M", a named method-development tier) failed adversarial review on specifics worth recording: a single-arm 36-case tier structurally cannot run two of the three defect-detection mechanisms that justify it (inversions and cross-arm disagreement need multiple arms; reachability is already a full-corpus static gate that subsetting would *degrade*); "effect direction stable across 2 repetitions" is a no-op within a session (12/12 bit-identical probes) and noise across sessions (23/48 flips); "relaxed rules" would punch a hole in the fail-closed registry — the exact unrecorded execution path the R6 guards were built to forbid; and a 36-case screen must never make adoption *or elimination* decisions (its MDE is ~25–31pp).

### 4.2 The recommendation: the two-level laboratory, correctly located

The two-level structure survives — as **decision rules and a registry state inside FIS, plus exactly one narrowly-scoped separate rig** — rather than as a named parallel system. Concretely:

1. **Consequence-bearing tolerances** (the real R6 fix). Every pre-registered tolerance names its breach consequence — ABORT / RECALIBRATE / PROCEED-WITH-DECLARED-CEILING(projected cost stated) — evaluated by curtailed exact counting (halt at the 4th cap-hit of 36) and enforced in-run by the runner, fail-closed, like the existing `--candidate` refusal.
2. **A SMOKE state in the R6 registry**, before REGISTERED: fixed 36 stratified cases (all 12 classes × 3, round-robin order), single arm, results written to the same append-only ledger and cryptographically marked non-promotable — minutes-scale detection of schema/tool-contract/runner breakage with no hole in the fail-closed machinery. This, not a "tier", is the small lab.
3. **Full-corpus static gates protected**: `make reachability`, gold-answer scoring, corpus determinism stay full-scale by rule; subsetting them is a regression.
4. **An elimination rule**: no candidate WITHDRAWN on a pilot margin below the pilot's own MDE (~9–11 of 36). Below it: both proceed under a pre-registered budget, the decision defers to DEV, or the selection metric changes to one the pilot can resolve. (R7 should explicitly un-withdraw UD-Q3_K_XL for re-selection — the R6 report already flags it.)
5. **A TEST-look ledger + Suite-v4 refresh trigger**, pre-registered now, before R7 spends look #8. Design note for v4 from §2.2 Finding 5: with ICC ≈ 0.4–0.6, power grows with the number of *classes* — v4 should add scenario classes (and FIS's task ontology has room) before adding replications.
6. **A prediction ledger**: each contract records a pre-run effect-size point estimate + interval; a standing predicted-vs-actual scorecard turns the "is this experiment worth DEV?" gate empirical within ~5 entries.
7. **One separate, throwaway rig — the fine-tuning pipeline** (the single place the separate-system steelman wins, because the thing to de-risk is the *training* pipeline FIS lacks, not the eval harness FIS has): smallest viable base model, ~200 synthetic examples, LoRA → merge → GGUF convert → quantize → serve → 12-case eval, target <30 min end-to-end; extend provenance with a `TrainedArtifact` record (dataset digest, base artifact id, hyperparameters, seed, adapter SHA, pipeline digests). Deleted once R8's mechanics are boring.

Graduation criteria that survive review: (i) zero harness/schema/tool-contract violations across the SMOKE cases, and (iii) a pre-registered contract (with consequence clauses and MDE statement) exists before any DEV spend. Criterion (ii) is deleted; criterion (iv) is replaced by the prediction ledger.

**Reasoning from first principles:** the lab's scarce resources are operator attention and measurement validity, not compute. A parallel system spends both to buy iteration speed the record shows is not binding; decision rules spend neither and fix every failure the record actually contains. **Evidence:** Appendix A's classification — every realism-dependent discovery (13 defects, R4's result, R5's null, Suite v3) required FIS; every process failure (cap burn, determinism boundary, clock skew) is fixed by a rule, not a system. **Strongest argument against (recorded honestly):** a shared harness shares blind spots — a defect in FIS's own scoring/verification machinery could sit undetected under both levels, where an independently implemented small system would provide implementation diversity; FIS's 13 defects were all found by FIS's *own* detection instruments, which is evidence those instruments work, not proof none remain. Mitigations: the frontier-saturation check (a suite the strongest arm can't saturate is suspect), inversion review, and the annual ML-Test-Score audit. **Conditions that would change the recommendation:** (a) a client engagement whose task shape FIS's investigation format doesn't transfer to (RAG-heavy retrieval QA, document workflows) → build the client-shaped testbed *then*, as the second realistic system; (b) corpus regeneration/complexity starts blocking >⅓ of experiment lead time (it is currently 3.8 s); (c) the SMOKE+rules regime itself proves gameable in practice (the prediction ledger would show it); (d) FIS stops being representative of the client systems the lab sells (sunk-cost audit re-runs at each client project).
